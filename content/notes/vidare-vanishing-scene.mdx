Vidare is a concept app for stranded SL travellers, shown inside a scroll-driven 3D Stockholm. It was running fine, and then roughly 12 to 15 seconds after the 3D appeared, the whole scene disappeared.

## The symptom

Healthy at first, gone after roughly 12 to 15 seconds, and an illustrated 2D fallback took its place. It happened every time on my M3 Mac, and a refresh always brought the 3D back for another few seconds. I first blamed scrolling, because I usually noticed it after scrolling down and back up.

## How I found it

Working with Claude Code, I started by taking scrolling out of the equation: we loaded the page in headless Chrome, didn't touch it, and logged the scene's quality setting every few seconds. The 3D canvas appeared at about 5 seconds, and at about 20 seconds the quality flipped to "fallback" and the canvas was removed. No scrolling involved. Something was running on a timer.

The only thing in the code that switches to the fallback on its own is the performance monitor: drei's `PerformanceMonitor`, which I'd set up with `flipflops={4}` and an `onFallback` that swaps in the illustration. The idea was to catch devices whose frame rate keeps swinging between good and bad.

Reading drei's source showed the problem. It adds to its flip-flop counter on every check that comes out "good" as well as every "bad" one, and it checks every 2.5 seconds. So on a healthy machine, five good checks in a row, about 12.5 seconds, were enough to cross my limit of four, and the 3D was swapped out.

The cause was the safety net itself. It counted perfectly healthy frames as instability and, after enough of them, swapped the 3D scene for the fallback.

## The fix

I removed `flipflops` and `onFallback`, so a healthy scene can never trip the fallback. Then I rebuilt the step-down for genuinely slow devices: below a fixed 24 fps it turns off effects first, then lowers the resolution in steps, and only gives up on 3D if it's still choppy at the lowest resolution.

I didn't trust "the tests pass". I confirmed the fix in a headless browser, running the scene past the point where it used to vanish: it stayed 3D for 45 seconds. I also simulated a genuinely slow device to check the fallback still kicks in when it should.

## What I took from it

- A safety net can become the bug. A fallback that triggers wrongly is worse than no fallback.
- For anything visual or timing-based, verify in a real browser, not only with unit tests.
- When a library's behaviour surprises you, read its source. Here, the answer was one line: a counter that went up on good news too.
